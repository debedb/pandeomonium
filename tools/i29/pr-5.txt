Warm painterly comic-book illustration in the same hand-drawn, richly coloured style as the reference panels (inked outlines, painted light). Setting: the 1972 Munich Olympic stadium on a bright afternoon, green pitch with white lines, sparse crowd, the stadium tent roof. The match is a parody "International Philosophy" football game: Germany versus Greece. Greek players wear white and pale-blue togas, sandals and laurel wreaths; German players wear 18th- and 19th-century dark frock coats, waistcoats and cravats. Absolutely no text, no letters, no numbers, no speech balloons, no readable writing anywhere. Keep the top fifth of the frame calm (space for lettering).

Panel: view from slightly behind and to one side of the German goal, looking out at the pitch through the corner of the white goal frame and net. Jesus, exactly as in the Jesus reference images (long brown hair, beard, golden halo, white robe with red sash, sandals), wearing padded goalkeeper gloves, is the goalkeeper standing on his goal line between the posts: he dives sideways at full stretch just under the crossbar and catches the ball cleanly in both gloved hands right in front of the net, serene and smiling. Out on the pitch a few metres away, Socrates (short Greek, curly brown beard, white toga) is still horizontal in the air at the end of a diving header, astonished. Greek and German philosophers in the background freeze in disbelief.
