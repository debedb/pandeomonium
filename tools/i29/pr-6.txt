Warm painterly comic-book illustration in the same hand-drawn, richly coloured style as the reference panels (inked outlines, painted light). Setting: the 1972 Munich Olympic stadium on a bright afternoon, green pitch with white lines, sparse crowd, the stadium tent roof. The match is a parody "International Philosophy" football game: Germany versus Greece. Greek players wear white and pale-blue togas, sandals and laurel wreaths; German players wear 18th- and 19th-century dark frock coats, waistcoats and cravats. Absolutely no text, no letters, no numbers, no speech balloons, no readable writing anywhere. Keep the top fifth of the frame calm (space for lettering).

Panel: point of view from the pitch looking straight at the German goal, so the goal and its net fill the background and we see the goalkeeper from the FRONT. Only two figures in the whole panel, no other players anywhere. Jesus, exactly as in the Jesus reference images (long wavy brown hair, brown beard, golden halo, white robe with red sash, sandals), wearing padded goalkeeper gloves, has leapt high off his line in front of the net and, in mid-air, catches the ball cleanly in both hands above his head, face serene and calm, robe and hair flying. In the near foreground, seen from behind and slightly to the side, Diego Maradona exactly as in the Maradona reference (1986: short, stocky and powerful, thick black curly hair, broad grinning face) in the light-blue-and-white vertically striped Argentina shirt and black shorts, but wearing Greek leather sandals instead of football boots (a number 10 on the back of his shirt is the only allowed marking) has jumped up against him and is punching at the ball with his raised LEFT fist, the fist just short of the ball. Empty stands-side grass and the stadium behind the goal.
